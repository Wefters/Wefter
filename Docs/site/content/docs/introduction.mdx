Wefter renders your app inside an Android WebView and gives that app a
direct line to native device features through a typed bridge between
JavaScript and Kotlin. You don't rewrite anything for mobile, and you don't
maintain a second codebase alongside your web app.

## Three tiers, and where Wefter sits

Native mobile frameworks generally fall into one of three architectural
categories. Wefter is built for one of them right now, and the other two
aren't on the roadmap as a shipped feature yet.

| Tier                     | What it means                                                                                                                                                     | Status                                   |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| 1. WebView + bridge      | Your app renders in a WebView. Native features like the camera, storage, or sensors are exposed as plugins your JS calls through `@wefter/core`'s `invokeNative`. | Built and shipped. This is Wefter today. |
| 2. Custom rendering      | Native `<canvas>` or surface rendering embedded next to the WebView, for graphics work the DOM handles badly.                                                     | Not built.                               |
| 3. Native widget driving | Your app controls real native UI widgets directly, skipping the WebView for specific screens.                                                                     | Not built.                               |

If your app needs Tier 2 or 3 today, Wefter won't get you there yet.

## How a native call actually works

When your JS calls `invokeNative('scanner', 'open', {})`, that call goes
straight to a Kotlin plugin on the native side and back, one hop each way.
There's no local server your UI is talking to and nothing running
underneath your app that needs to start up before a call can go through.
Your app is JS in a WebView, plus whatever native plugins you've declared.

That's also why the [splash screen](/docs/app-splash-screen) is optional
and off by default. There's no framework startup cost to cover, so when you
do configure a splash, it's there for branding, not because the app needs
the time.
